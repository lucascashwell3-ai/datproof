Five pillars. 100 points. Every one a sourced fact.

Four pillars score the evidence behind the coins; the fifth scores the balance sheet carrying them, because fixed obligations against a volatile asset thin the margin that evidence sits on. Nothing is estimated, and where DATproof hasn’t sourced something it says so instead of scoring it. Current field: B C D F. Anyone can re-run every grade from the public registry.

On-chain proof

40 pts

Something a reader can check on the chain themselves: published wallet addresses, or the outputs the company controls with signatures proving that control. All-or-nothing — existence is either independently checkable or it isn't. An A is impossible without it.

Disclosure quality

30 pts

Regulatory filings carry liability for misstatement; press releases don't. Scored from the registry's evidence tiers — filings earn full marks, statements partial, third-party attribution none.

Independent attestation

10 pts

A disclosed third-party check on custodian balances narrows the trust gap — credited, and capped, because the referees themselves warn it is not assurance.

Disclosure freshness

10 pts

A balance is a point-in-time claim that decays daily. Full marks inside 45 days, partial to 120, none beyond — measured against the registry snapshot, so no grade ever falls just because time passed on DATproof's side.

Balance-sheet resilience

10 pts

Fixed obligations serviced against a volatile asset thin the evidence's margin for error. Clean structure earns full marks.

Why grade proof at all?

Bitcoin is the first treasury asset whose ownership anyone can verify — and almost no company uses that. “The reasons are real, and worth stating fairly”: custody confidentiality, security concerns, and no rule requiring it. Who made this, and how to correct it.

A page that grades named companies owes them a way to answer back, and owes its readers a statement of its own interests. Both are here rather than left to be discovered.

Who

One person, not an institution: Lucas Cashwell, a risk-and-controls background in financial services. “Rating agency” describes what the site does, not its size. The method is public precisely so nobody has to take the author’s word either — the rubric, the registry and the engine are all in one open repository.

Corrections and right of reply

A graded company that believes a figure, date, tier or letter is wrong can open a correction request. Requests are public and timestamped, answered within five business days, and a correction that changes a grade is noted on the page with its date. Evidence submitted — a filing reference, an attestation report, published addresses — is applied to the registry the same way as anything else.

Interests

DATproof takes no advertising, no sponsorship and no payment from any rated company or proof-of-reserves vendor, and none has ever been offered or accepted. The author has a risk-and-controls background in financial services and is moving into crypto-native risk and compliance — a professional interest that the visibility of this project serves, stated here rather than left to be discovered.
